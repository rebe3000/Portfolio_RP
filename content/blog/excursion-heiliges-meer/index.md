Application of various biogeochemical methods: flow chamber measurements, Los
Gatos measuring device, exchange coefficient, daily CO₂ fluctuations, flow
measurements in the dark and in the light, incubation, dialysis chambers,
photometry, depth profile, sediment traps, and multi-probe for depth profile.

Five million km² of the Earth’s land surface is covered by inland waters
measured from a size of 0.002 km². This corresponds to around 3.7 per cent of
the total land surface. These lakes are dominated by small, shallow lakes.
Until now, it was assumed that large lakes accounted for a greater proportion,
and small lakes were therefore neglected.

The measurement methods learned and applied in this excursion are useful to
understand gas fluxes from small, shallow lakes, as well as the gas cycles and
processes involved, as the emissions from small lakes may make a significant
contribution to climate change.
